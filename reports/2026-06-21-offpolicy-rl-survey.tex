\begin{abstract}\noindent
A working reference for the off-policy actor--critic family, written to inform the DDPG/TD3 implementation on
the HyMeKo cart-pole (PPO is the saved baseline). It traces the lineage DDPG\,$\to$\,TD3\,$\to$\,SAC by the
\emph{specific failure each successor fixes}, surveys the modern sample-efficiency/stability methods (REDQ,
TQC, DroQ, CrossQ, TD7), distils the design axes that actually matter, maps each onto the repo's
\code{RolloutEnv}\,+\,backbone\,+\,exploration-vocab\,+\,off-policy-core architecture, and sketches how safe RL
(constrained MDPs) would enter declaratively. Synthesis from the established literature (knowledge to
2026-01); not a web-cited survey --- a deep-research pass can add external citations on request.
\end{abstract}

\section{The off-policy contract}
On-policy methods (PPO, our baseline) collect a fresh rollout, update, and discard it. Off-policy methods keep
a \textbf{replay buffer} of past transitions $(s,a,r,s',d)$ and resample them, decoupling data collection from
the update --- far more sample-efficient, at the cost of stability. The shared machinery:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{A $Q$-critic} $Q_\phi(s,a)$ (action-value), trained by a Bellman/TD target
        $y = r + \gamma\,(1-d)\,Q_{\bar\phi}(s', a')$ on a \emph{target} network $\bar\phi$ (Polyak-averaged,
        $\bar\phi \leftarrow \tau\phi + (1-\tau)\bar\phi$) for a non-moving regression target.
  \item \textbf{A policy} $\pi$ improved \emph{through} the critic, not by a likelihood-ratio surrogate.
  \item \textbf{Exploration} injected explicitly (the policy is deterministic or its entropy is controlled),
        not implicit in the on-policy stochasticity.
\end{itemize}
The recurring enemy is \textbf{$Q$-value overestimation}: $\max/\arg\max$ over a noisy critic biases the
target upward, the policy exploits the error, and training diverges. Most of the lineage is a sequence of
overestimation controls.

\section{DDPG --- deterministic policy gradient}
\textbf{Idea.} A deterministic actor $\mu_\theta(s)$ and a $Q$-critic; the actor ascends the critic:
\[
\nabla_\theta J \approx \mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[\nabla_a Q_\phi(s,a)\big|_{a=\mu_\theta(s)}\,
\nabla_\theta \mu_\theta(s)\big].
\]
Critic regresses to $y = r + \gamma\,(1-d)\,Q_{\bar\phi}(s',\mu_{\bar\theta}(s'))$. Exploration $=$ additive
noise at action selection: $a = \mu_\theta(s) + \epsilon$, $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}$ or Ornstein--Uhlenbeck.
\textbf{Failure modes (why nobody ships plain DDPG):} severe $Q$-overestimation; extreme sensitivity to
hyperparameters/seed (the cart-pole's MLP-bimodality is a mild echo); brittle target updates. It is the right
\emph{first} implementation (smallest off-policy core) but expected to be fragile --- report that honestly
rather than tune it away.

\section{TD3 --- three fixes to DDPG}
TD3 $=$ DDPG $+$ three targeted patches, each addressing a named DDPG pathology:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Clipped double-$Q$ (twin critics).} Two critics $Q_{\phi_1},Q_{\phi_2}$; the target uses the
        \emph{minimum}: $y = r + \gamma(1-d)\min_i Q_{\bar\phi_i}(s',\tilde a')$. Directly caps overestimation.
  \item \textbf{Delayed policy updates.} Update the actor (and targets) every $d$ critic steps ($d{=}2$) ---
        a slower-moving policy on a better-fit critic.
  \item \textbf{Target policy smoothing.} $\tilde a' = \mu_{\bar\theta}(s') + \mathrm{clip}(\epsilon,-c,c)$,
        $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma)$ --- regularises the critic so it can't exploit sharp peaks.
\end{enumerate}
TD3 is the reliable deterministic baseline; on our codebase it is \emph{additive over DDPG} (a second critic,
a counter, one more noise site --- the third injection site in the exploration vocab).

\section{SAC --- maximum-entropy, stochastic, off-policy}
\textbf{Idea.} Maximise reward \emph{plus} policy entropy: $J = \sum_t \mathbb{E}[r_t + \alpha\,
\mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot|s_t))]$. A \emph{stochastic} squashed-Gaussian actor (tanh-Gaussian) gives the
reparameterised policy gradient; soft $Q$ targets add the entropy term; twin critics (as TD3) cap
overestimation; the temperature $\alpha$ is auto-tuned to a target entropy. \textbf{Why it matters:} SAC is the
single strongest, most robust continuous-control baseline --- the fair opponent to put the HSiKAN backbone into.
It is also the cleanest fit for the exploration vocab: entropy is intrinsic (a source wired to the policy
distribution), so PPO and SAC share that injection site while differing in the on/off-policy core.

\section{Newer architectures (post-SAC: sample efficiency \& stability)}
The frontier is mostly \emph{better/faster critics} at high update-to-data (UTD) ratios:
\begin{description}[nosep,leftmargin=1.0cm]
  \item[REDQ (2021).] A \emph{randomised ensemble} of $N$ critics; targets take the min over a random subset
        of size $M{=}2$. Enables UTD$\,\approx\,$20 (many gradient steps per env step) $\Rightarrow$ SAC-level
        returns at a fraction of the samples. Cost: $N$ critics.
  \item[TQC (2020).] \emph{Truncated Quantile Critics} --- distributional critics (quantile regression) whose
        pooled atoms are \emph{truncated} (drop the top-$k$) to control overestimation continuously rather than
        by a hard min. Strong on harder MuJoCo.
  \item[DroQ (2021).] REDQ's effect with a \emph{small} ensemble by adding \textbf{dropout + LayerNorm} to the
        critics --- cheaper, nearly as sample-efficient. A pragmatic default.
  \item[CrossQ (2024).] \textbf{Removes target networks entirely}; uses BatchNorm (carefully placed) + a wider
        critic to stay stable. Simpler and compute-light, matching REDQ-class efficiency --- attractive because
        it deletes the target-net machinery.
  \item[TD7 (2023).] TD3 $+$ \textbf{SALE} (state--action learned embeddings: a learned latent the critic/actor
        consume) $+$ \textbf{LAP} (loss-adjusted prioritised replay) $+$ policy checkpoints. State of the art
        among deterministic methods; the SALE embedding is conceptually close to our \emph{backbone} idea.
\end{description}
Adjacent families worth knowing, not on the immediate path: \textbf{distributional RL} (C51\,$\to$\,QR-DQN\,
$\to$\,IQN --- TQC's lineage), \textbf{model-based} (MBPO, Dreamer-v3 --- learn a world model, plan/imagine),
and \textbf{offline RL} (CQL, IQL --- learn from a fixed buffer, no interaction).

\section{The design axes that actually matter}
A taxonomy to place any method (and any HyMeKo variant) on:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
axis & options & who \\
\midrule
data & on-policy / off-policy(replay) & PPO / DDPG,TD3,SAC,\dots \\
policy & deterministic / stochastic & DDPG,TD3 / SAC,PPO \\
critic & $V(s)$ / $Q(s,a)$ / distributional $Z(s,a)$ & PPO / DDPG\dots / TQC,IQN \\
overestimation ctrl & min-of-2 / ensemble-min / quantile-trunc & TD3 / REDQ / TQC \\
target net & Polyak / none & most / CrossQ \\
exploration site & policy-entropy / action-noise / target-smoothing & PPO,SAC / DDPG,TD3 / TD3 \\
UTD ratio & 1 / high($\sim$20) & classic / REDQ,DroQ \\
representation & raw / learned embedding (SALE) / \textbf{hypergraph (HSiKAN)} & most / TD7 / \textbf{ours} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The last row is the research seam: every method above is agnostic to \emph{how the state is encoded}, so the
HSiKAN hypergraph backbone is an orthogonal swap into any of them --- which is exactly why a real-topology
task (not cart-pole) is needed to test whether the structure helps.

\section{Mapping onto the HyMeKo substrate}
The repo already has the orthogonal pieces; off-policy adds a bounded core (architecture plan
\code{2026-06-21-rl-algorithm-architecture}):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Reuse:} \code{RolloutEnv} Protocol (collection), the \code{hsikan}/\code{mlp}/\code{signedkan}
        backbones (now also feeding a $Q$-critic that consumes \code{concat(}embed\code{, action)}), the
        vectorised env factory, \code{policy\_store} (off-policy policies store identically).
  \item \textbf{New (P2):} \code{ReplayBuffer} (SoA ring, fixed RSS), \code{QCritic} + deterministic actor
        head, target nets + Polyak, the DPG update. \textbf{P3} (TD3) is additive: twin $Q$, delayed updates,
        target smoothing. \textbf{Later:} SAC (stochastic head + auto-$\alpha$), then a REDQ/CrossQ critic
        option behind a flag.
  \item \textbf{Exploration vocab (P1):} the three injection sites (policy-dist, action-select, target-action)
        already cover PPO/DDPG/TD3/SAC; a method \emph{declares} which sites it consumes. DDPG can ship with a
        minimal Gaussian \code{NoiseProcess} now and be retrofitted to the declarative vocab.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Recommended order:} DDPG (smallest core, expect fragility) $\to$ TD3 (the reliable deterministic
baseline) $\to$ SAC (the strong stochastic baseline) $\to$ optionally a high-UTD critic (DroQ/CrossQ) for
sample efficiency. Each benchmarked against the saved PPO reference (192$\pm$15 cart-pole), and the
\emph{architecture} comparison run only on a real-topology task with the params-matched control.

\section{Safe reinforcement learning --- the next direction}
Once an off-policy learner works, ``safe RL'' asks it to respect constraints, not just maximise reward.
The standard framings:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Constrained MDP (CMDP).} Maximise return subject to expected cost $\le$ budget:
        $\max_\pi J_r(\pi)\ \text{s.t.}\ J_c(\pi)\le d$. Solved by a \textbf{Lagrangian} (a learned multiplier
        $\lambda$ on the cost, dual ascent) --- e.g.\ Lagrangian-SAC/TD3, or \textbf{CPO} (a trust-region
        constrained policy step).
  \item \textbf{Safety layer / shielding.} A projection or shield that overrides unsafe actions at execution
        (a hard constraint), optionally learned (Safety-Layer) or model-based (control-barrier functions).
  \item \textbf{Recovery / fallback policies.} A separate policy that activates near constraint violation.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{The HyMeKo angle (why this is a natural fit):} constraints are \emph{declarative} here already. The
reward is a weighted bundle of \code{meta\_reward} terms; a \textbf{cost} is the same shape (a
\code{meta\_constraint} vocabulary: a bundle of cost terms with budgets), and the kinematic hypergraph already
carries the safety-relevant structure (joint limits, self-collision, out-of-bounds --- terms we \emph{already}
have as reward penalties in \code{galambos\_task}). So a CMDP would be: read a \code{constraint\_spec} from
\code{.hymeko}, evaluate $J_c$ from those terms, and add a Lagrangian multiplier to the off-policy update ---
the exact same ``MDP-and-now-its-constraints as data'' story. \emph{Proposed as the direction after TD3; not
started.}

\section{Bottom line for the implementation}
Build DDPG now as the minimal off-policy core (honest about its fragility), TD3 immediately after as the
deterministic baseline, then SAC as the strong opponent. Keep the HSiKAN backbone as an orthogonal swap, and
hold the architecture verdict for a real-topology task. Safe RL enters as a declarative \code{meta\_constraint}
$+$ a Lagrangian on the off-policy update --- the cleanest continuation of the storage thesis.
