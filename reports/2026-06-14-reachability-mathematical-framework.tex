\begin{abstract}
We isolate one algebraic object --- a \emph{reachability rule}: a parameter that
selects which generators seed a monotone closure operator, evaluated against an
admissibility predicate --- and show it instantiates, with the same monotonicity
and reduction theorems, in three otherwise-unrelated programs implemented in the
HyMeKo framework: (A) Friedler-axiom process-network synthesis (P-graph
optimization: MSG/SSG/ABB), (B) signed-link-prediction leakage auditing (neural
networks), and (C) gatekeeper-bounded cognitive-agent synthesis (AKOIRE). The
contribution is twofold: a small theory (\S\ref{sec:theory}) with proofs, and
three instantiations (\S\ref{sec:pgraph}--\S\ref{sec:agent}) that each become a
standalone article. We are explicit (\S\ref{sec:status}) about what is a theorem,
what is an empirically supported proposition, and what is a conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Three problems in this codebase share a structure that is usually re-derived ad
hoc in each. (A) PNS synthesis asks which products are \emph{producible} from a
set of operating units --- a forward closure gated by the five Friedler axioms.
(B) The signed-link leakage audit asks whether a held-out edge sign is
\emph{reachable} by a model's message passing --- a closure over an adjacency,
the basis of the strict vs.\ transductive protocols. (C) The AKOIRE cognitive
loop asks which refinements of an always-parseable \emph{ambience} are
\emph{reachable} under a gatekeeper --- a closure over accepted edits, bounded by
a round cap. In all three a closure is computed, its seed/generator set is a free
parameter, and an admissibility predicate filters the result. We name that
parameter and study it once.

\section{The theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Closures and rules}

Let $(L,\subseteq)$ be a finite lattice (in practice the powerset of a ground set
of edges, materials, or refinements).

\begin{definition}[Generator-seeded closure]
A \emph{closure family} is a monotone operator
$\Cl : 2^{G}\times L \to L$, where $G$ is a set of \emph{generators} and, for
fixed generator set $\Gamma\subseteq G$, $\Cl(\Gamma,\cdot)$ is a closure
operator: extensive ($X\subseteq \Cl(\Gamma,X)$), monotone
($X\subseteq Y \Rightarrow \Cl(\Gamma,X)\subseteq\Cl(\Gamma,Y)$), and idempotent.
We additionally require monotonicity in the generators:
$\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma' \Rightarrow \Cl(\Gamma,X)\subseteq\Cl(\Gamma',X)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reachability rule]
\label{def:rule}
A \emph{reachability rule} $R$ is a map assigning to a problem instance a
generator set $\Gamma_R\subseteq G$. The \emph{reachable set} under $R$ from a
base $B$ is $\reach_R(B) := \Cl(\Gamma_R, B)$. Rules are ordered by
$R\sqsubseteq R'$ iff $\Gamma_R\subseteq\Gamma_{R'}$ on every instance.
\end{definition}

In every instantiation below the same three rules appear, forming a chain:
\begin{equation}
  \Rstrict \;\sqsubseteq\; \Rtopo \;\sqsubseteq\; \Rfull .
  \label{eq:lattice}
\end{equation}
$\Rstrict$ admits only \emph{confirmed} generators (training edges / established
units / committed ambience); $\Rtopo$ additionally admits \emph{candidate
connectivity} with labels withheld; $\Rfull$ admits candidates with labels.

\subsection{Core results}

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity]
\label{thm:mono}
For any base $B$, $R\sqsubseteq R' \Rightarrow \reach_R(B)\subseteq\reach_{R'}(B)$.
In particular, along \eqref{eq:lattice},
$\reach_{\Rstrict}(B)\subseteq\reach_{\Rtopo}(B)\subseteq\reach_{\Rfull}(B)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$R\sqsubseteq R'$ gives $\Gamma_R\subseteq\Gamma_{R'}$ by
Definition~\ref{def:rule}; apply generator-monotonicity of $\Cl$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reduction]
\label{thm:reduction}
Let $\Cl_0$ be a domain's canonical closure with its conventional generator set
$\Gamma_0$. If $\Gamma_{\Rstrict}=\Gamma_0$, then
$\reach_{\Rstrict}(B)=\Cl_0(B)$ for all $B$: the strict rule reproduces the
pre-existing semantics exactly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from $\Cl(\Gamma_{\Rstrict},B)=\Cl(\Gamma_0,B)=\Cl_0(B)$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} is the safety guarantee: introducing the rule axis
does not perturb a domain's established results --- it is a conservative
extension, checked by a regression test in each instantiation.

\begin{theorem}[Bound admissibility]
\label{thm:admiss}
Let a branch-and-bound search prune a branch when a feasibility target
$T\subseteq G$ is not contained in an \emph{optimistic} reachable set
$\reach_{R}(B^{\uparrow})$ (the closure under the maximal still-available
generators). Widening $R$ to $R'\sqsupseteq R$ keeps the bound \emph{admissible}:
no branch pruned under $R'$ would have been retained under $R$, and the search
remains exact.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}, $\reach_{R}(B^{\uparrow})\subseteq
\reach_{R'}(B^{\uparrow})$, so $T\subseteq\reach_{R}(B^{\uparrow})\Rightarrow
T\subseteq\reach_{R'}(B^{\uparrow})$: the wider rule prunes a (weak) superset of
the branches the narrower one keeps feasible, never cutting an optimum. Exactness
of the underlying B\&B is preserved because the leaf feasibility test is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\section{Instantiation A --- Process-graph optimization}
\label{sec:pgraph}

\textbf{Closure.} $G$ = operating units; $B=\Gamma_0$ = raw materials $R$;
$\Cl$ = the producibility fixpoint \texttt{close\_producible}: admit a unit's
outputs once all its inputs are reachable. This is exactly the closure the ABB
reachability bound and SSG feasibility evaluate (\texttt{hymeko\_pgraph}).

\textbf{Rules.} $\Gamma_{\Rstrict}$ = confirmed units (canonical PNS, so
$\Gamma_{\Rstrict}=\Gamma_0$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} applies);
$\Rtopo/\Rfull$ additionally admit \emph{candidate} units, differing only in
whether candidate \emph{costs} are counted --- the cost layer, orthogonal to the
closure.

\textbf{Axioms as a second filter.} The Friedler axioms S1--S5 are an
admissibility predicate on the closed structure; A4/S4 (``every operating unit
reaches a required product'') is itself a reachability statement, and the
extension bundle (\texttt{axiom\_extensions.rs}) shows orthogonal rules already
sit \emph{alongside} S1--S5 without contradiction. The reachability rule is the
\emph{reachability-axis} analogue: a refinement \emph{regime} (the
\texttt{regime.rs} Strategy seam) layered on MSG/SSG/ABB.

\emph{Verified (Rust, \texttt{hymeko\_pgraph::reachability}):}
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} as a unit test ($\Rstrict$ equals
\texttt{close\_producible}); Theorem~\ref{thm:mono} as the monotone-closure test;
a fixture where a product is producible \emph{only} via a candidate unit
(reachability strictly grows). Article~A (\S\ref{sec:articles}).

\section{Instantiation B --- Signed-link leakage (neural networks)}
\label{sec:nn}

\textbf{Closure.} $G$ = edges (with optional signs); $B$ = the node set;
$\Cl$ = the message-passing reachability of a signed GNN/transformer --- a node's
representation reaches the labels on edges in its receptive field.

\textbf{Rules.} $\Gamma_{\Rstrict}$ = training edges only; $\Rtopo$ adds
test-edge \emph{topology} with signs masked to a neutral token; $\Rfull$ adds
test edges \emph{with} signs (the convention these baselines self-report under).
A train-label \emph{shuffle} permutes training signs; the held-out AUROC under
shuffle is the leakage probe.

\begin{definition}[Leak]
A method \emph{leaks} under $(R,\text{shuffle})$ if its held-out AUROC under the
shuffle stays above chance.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Necessity]
\label{prop:nec}
If the held-out label is not in $\reach_R(B)$ then the method does not leak under
$(R,\text{shuffle})$ (the readout has no access to it).
\end{proposition}
Under $\Rstrict$ the test sign is never reachable, so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:nec} the strict-shuffle AUROC is chance. \emph{Verified
empirically:} 5-seed grid, $7$ baselines $\times 5$ datasets ($350$ runs); mean
shuffled AUROC $0.515$--$0.545$, $\sim$35\,pp below real; epinions $0.500$,
slashdot $0.514$ (clean to chance).

\begin{proposition}[Reachability is not sufficient: the readout factor]
\label{prop:readout}
Label reachability under $R$ is necessary but not sufficient for a leak.
Leakage factors as
\[
  \textsc{leak}(R,\text{model}) \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \big[\text{label}\in\reach_R(B)\big]\ \wedge\ \big[\text{readout is local}\big],
\]
where a \emph{local} readout exposes a held-out edge's own label environment to
its per-edge logit, and a \emph{diffuse} (node-embedding) readout averages it
away.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Empirical support.} Under $\Rfull$ (test signs reachable), an SGT with a
node-embedding $\times$ MLP readout still drops to chance under shuffle
($0.514$, bitcoin-alpha, 60 ep) --- reachable but not exploited; whereas a
cycle-/path-feature readout leaks at $0.73$ (2026-06-11). The factorization is
the proposed headline of Article~B; the decisive test is the $\Rtopo$ sweep
across readout families (wired and unit-tested; sweep pending).

\begin{remark}[A residual the label-shuffle misses]
On reddit-body the strict-shuffle AUROC is a \emph{stable} $0.564\pm0.024$, not
chance. Because the shuffle permutes signs but preserves topology, a node-level
sign prior carried by degree/position survives it. Formally this is leakage
through a \emph{generator the rule does not vary} (topology); isolating it needs a
degree-preserving rewire control. (Conjecture~\ref{conj:topo}.)
\end{remark}

\section{Instantiation C --- Cognitive-agent synthesis (AKOIRE)}
\label{sec:agent}

\textbf{Closure.} $G$ = candidate refinements the agent may propose;
$B$ = the current \emph{ambience} (an always-parseable source with a monotonic
generation counter); $\Cl$ = the set of ambiences reachable by sequences of
\emph{gatekeeper-accepted} refinements. Each accepted refinement increments the
generation; the closure is approached one step per loop round, bounded by
\texttt{max\_rounds}.

\textbf{Rule \& admissibility.} The gatekeeper (the HyMeKo Layer-1 parser) is the
admissibility predicate: a refinement is admitted iff the resulting source parses
(the cognitive-layer analogue of the Friedler axioms). The reachability rule
selects which refinements are candidates --- a \emph{strict} agent reaches only
refinements over confirmed structure; a \emph{transductive} agent may reach over
proposed-but-unconfirmed structure, with or without committing its semantics
(topology vs.\ full), exactly mirroring \eqref{eq:lattice}.

\begin{proposition}[Termination]
\label{prop:term}
Since the generation counter is strictly monotone on acceptance and the loop is
capped, the cognitive closure is approached by a finite, non-decreasing chain
$B=A_0\sqsubseteq A_1\sqsubseteq\cdots$ terminating in
\textsc{Converged}, \textsc{Exhausted}, or \textsc{CapReached}.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Realized} in \texttt{akoire} (the \texttt{CognitiveLoop} driver,
\texttt{ScriptedSynthesizer} for determinism); the LLM-backed synthesizer is the
production binding. Article~C frames the agent as \emph{reachable-refinement
synthesis under a structural gatekeeper} --- ``structurally accountable AI'': the
agent's moves are exactly the admissible elements of a reachability closure.

\section{Epistemic status (honesty)}
\label{sec:status}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Theorems \ref{thm:mono}--\ref{thm:admiss}} are proved above and hold
in every instantiation; \ref{thm:reduction} is additionally a passing regression
test in domains A and B.
\item \textbf{Propositions \ref{prop:nec}--\ref{prop:term}} are correct given
their definitions; \ref{prop:readout} (the readout factor) is
\emph{empirically supported}, not proved --- it rests on the SGT-vs-cycle
contrast and awaits the full $\Rtopo$ sweep.
\item The \textbf{cognitive-agent} instantiation (\S\ref{sec:agent}) is the least
formally developed: the closure/gatekeeper picture is faithful to the
\texttt{akoire} implementation, but ``reachable-refinement synthesis'' is a
framing, not yet a theorem about agent competence.
\end{itemize}

\begin{conjecture}[Topology-carried residual]
\label{conj:topo}
On graphs where edge sign correlates with node topological position
(e.g.\ reddit-body), held-out AUROC under $(\Rstrict,\text{shuffle})$ exceeds
chance by a margin equal to the predictivity of a degree/position-only node
prior; a degree-preserving rewire of the test topology removes it.
\end{conjecture}
