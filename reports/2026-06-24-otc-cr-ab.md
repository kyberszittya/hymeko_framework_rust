# OTC link-sign: Catmull-Rom vs B-spline A/B ("OTC should use CR")

**Date:** 2026-06-24 · **Branch:** soma-vision · **SHA:** 292388b (working tree dirty — the signed_kan unification
session) · **CPU**, `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1`, 2 BLAS/OMP threads.

## Question
HSiKAN's identity is the **Catmull-Rom (CR)** KAN spline, but the OTC deployment recipe used **B-spline**. Does CR
match B-spline on Bitcoin-OTC link-sign prediction? (User: "OTC should use CR — that's the whole point.")

## Method
The *same* general HSiKAN architecture (`signed_kan.SignedGraphHSiKAN`: transductive node embeddings → pairwise
signed message passing + highway → `EdgeSignHead`), changing **only the edge activation** (`cr` vs `bspline`, both
re-homed in `signed_kan.splines`). Identical data + split via the legacy loader (`signedkan_wip.src.datasets.legacy.
load`/`split`, 80/10/10). The message-passing adjacency is built from **train edges only** — no test-label leakage.
Harness: `signedkan_wip/experiments/otc_cr_ab.py`. 200 epochs, Adam(lr 1e-2, wd 1e-5), hidden 32, 2 layers,
`skip="highway"`, seeds {0,1,2}. Eval on held-out test edges: AUC (positive = `+1`), macro-F1, binary-F1.

## Result (mean ± std over 3 seeds)

| activation | AUC | macro-F1 | binary-F1 | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Catmull-Rom** | **0.9054 ± 0.0056** | 0.7869 ± 0.0039 | 0.963 | **14.9 s** |
| B-spline | 0.9054 ± 0.0046 | 0.7916 ± 0.0073 | 0.963 | 97.5 s |

Per-seed AUC — CR: 0.9133 / 0.9024 / 0.9007; B-spline: 0.9116 / 0.9039 / 0.9008.

## Conclusion
- **AUC is a dead tie** — 0.9054 for both, to four decimals; the per-seed gaps (≤ 0.0017) are well inside the
  cross-seed std.
- **macro-F1**: B-spline +0.0047, inside the overlapping std (CR wins seed 2; B-spline wins seeds 0,1) — noise.
- **CR is 6.6× faster** (14.9 s vs 97.5 s / 200 epochs) — its closed-form blend vs B-spline's Cox–de Boor
  recursion. The defining HSiKAN spline costs **nothing on quality and saves most of the compute**.

**"OTC should use CR" is validated in both architectures:** in the general pairwise HSiKAN CR *ties* B-spline on
AUC at ~6.6× less compute; in the legacy triad architecture (bare train) CR *beats* B-spline by +0.033 AUC with
lower variance and ~6.5× less compute (see the legacy section below). Either way, the HSiKAN-defining spline costs
nothing on quality — and often helps — while saving most of the compute. The intuition holds.

## Legacy triad architecture — head-to-head (added 2026-06-24)
The caveat below is now addressed: the *legacy* k-uniform-triad layer (`signedkan_wip.src.core.train`, which I
extended with an additive `spline_kind` flag) was run on OTC, CR vs B-spline, 3 seeds × 100 epochs, same loader.

| activation | AUC | macro-F1 | wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Catmull-Rom** | **0.6350 ± 0.0055** | 0.4735 ± 0.0014 | **110 s** |
| B-spline | 0.6023 ± 0.0199 | 0.4535 ± 0.0147 | 712 s |

Per-seed AUC Δ (CR − B-spline): +0.020 / +0.024 / +0.054 — **CR wins all three seeds**, by **+0.033 AUC on
average**, with **lower variance** (0.006 vs 0.020) and **6.5× faster**. So in the triad architecture CR does not
just tie — it *beats* B-spline here.

**Important regime caveat:** this is the **bare `train()`** (a plain `SignedKAN`, ~0.60–0.64 AUC), **not** the full
HighwaySignedKAN recipe (EC + R2 + spectral-entropy + tuned init) that produces the published 0.8738. The CR edge
is measured in this *under-fit* regime, where the spline choice plausibly matters more; whether it survives the
full recipe (both may converge higher, closing or keeping the gap) is the natural next experiment.

## Full HighwaySignedKAN recipe — GPU, the definitive run (added 2026-06-24)
Run on an RTX 3070 (the full recipe segfaults on the Windows-CPU torch sparse-CSR beta; it is the harness's
intended CUDA path). `run_highway_catmull_compare`, OTC, 3 seeds × 200 epochs, EC + R2 + spectral-entropy + tuned
init — the recipe that produces the published ~0.8738.

| activation | AUC | macro-F1 | train | inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Catmull-Rom | 0.8689 ± 0.0092 | 0.7673 ± 0.0101 | 51 s | 88 ms |
| B-spline | 0.8639 ± 0.0134 | 0.7659 ± 0.0145 | 67 s | 100 ms |

Per-seed AUC — CR: 0.8812 / 0.8590 / 0.8667; B-spline: 0.8786 / 0.8463 / 0.8668. Both reproduce ~0.87 (the
published level), confirming the recipe + GPU path.

**Verdict: a tie.** CR is +0.005 AUC, well inside the overlapping std. **The bare-train "+0.033 CR edge" does NOT
survive convergence** — it was an under-fit-regime artifact. CR is modestly cheaper (~1.3× train, ~1.14× inference);
the earlier "54×" was init-amortization in a 10-epoch smoke (B-spline ran first, paid CUDA init; CR ran warm), not
a real per-epoch ratio.

**Overall conclusion across all settings (pairwise general HSiKAN, legacy bare-train, full recipe):** CR is
**equivalent** to B-spline on OTC AUC at proper convergence, and modestly faster/simpler. "OTC should use CR" is
justified on **equivalence + simplicity + CR being HSiKAN's defining spline** — *not* on superior accuracy.

## Caveats (what this is NOT)
- The **pairwise** table is the general HSiKAN with a train-only-adjacency, no-leakage protocol; the **legacy
  triad** table is the bare `train()` (not the published full recipe). Neither ~0.905 nor ~0.635 is a claim about
  the published 0.8738 — they are clean **within-architecture** CR-vs-B-spline comparisons in two settings.
- 3 seeds, single hyper-parameter point (no sweep). Enough to settle the comparison + the speed gap; not a full
  study. The full-recipe (HighwaySignedKAN) CR-vs-B-spline run is the recommended follow-up.
