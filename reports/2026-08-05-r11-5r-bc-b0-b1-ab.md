## Question

R11.4B found conditioned-BC `BC_REPRESENTATION_INSUFFICIENT` with **two** measured causes: (1) an
under-determined descriptor→θ map from 44 demos (the *dominant* cause — smooth regressors fail even
in-distribution and 1-NN retrieval only 25 % held-out), and (2) ~1/3 of certified deliveries sit in
**narrow/chaotic θ-basins** (a 1e-5 θ change moved 7.99 → 54.27 mm), capping any approximate policy.

R11.5R re-certified the delivery bank for wide-basin θ (`RECERTIFICATION_PASS`, 49/56 wide, survival
0.708 → 0.958). This A/B asks the sufficiency question: **feeding the wider-basin targets into the
*same* BC harness (single variable — only the target θ changes), does closed-loop generalization
improve?** B0 = nominal R11.4B dataset; B1 = robust dataset (49/56 θ differ). Same 44/7/5 split,
same `r11_4b_conditioned_bc` harness, deterministic (`torch.manual_seed(0)`).

**B0 reproduces the published R11.4B numbers exactly** (mean-θ held .167, nearest .250, ridge/mlp
.167, frozen-R2 .000) — so every B1 delta is attributable to the robust targets alone.

## Interpretation — the two R11.4B causes are now cleanly separated and independently attributed

**Cause 2 (narrow basins) was real and is REMOVED by B1.**
- Train fit ridge/mlp **+6/44** (17 → 23), mean-θ **+0.159**: wider-basin targets are genuinely more
  learnable in-distribution — a smoother, more approximable θ-field.
- The **crude policies generalize better** on held-out: mean-θ 2 → 4, nearest 3 → 4. A constant
  (train-mean) θ or a nearest-neighbour θ lands strict-K6 on **more unseen scenarios** simply because
  the robust basins are wide enough to tolerate the approximation. This is exactly the mechanism the
  robustness re-certification was designed to buy, and it shows up on held-out.

**Cause 1 (under-determined descriptor→θ map) is NOT addressed by basin width — it persists.**
- The **parametric** regressors ridge/mlp are **flat on held-out** (2 → 2). No matter how wide the
  target basins are, a smooth regressor still cannot generalize the descriptor→θ map to unseen
  scenarios from 44 demos. Basin width and map-coverage are **orthogonal** failure axes; R11.5R fixes
  the former and leaves the latter exactly where R11.4B found it.

**Striking inversion:** on held-out B1, the crude baselines (nearest .333, mean-θ .333) now **beat
the learned MLP** (.167). With robust targets you do not need — and are hurt by — the smooth
regressor; retrieval/averaging of a wide-basin θ generalizes better than fitting a map the data
cannot pin down.

---

## Decision (per the pre-registered R11.5R tree)

> "does ridge/MLP closed-loop GENERALIZE from wider targets? **Only THEN** RL from wider warm-start."

Ridge/MLP held-out is **flat** → **RL-from-wider-warm-start is NOT greenlit.** A TD3 warm-start from
the MLP would inherit a policy that is no better than R11.4B's on held-out; wider basins do not help
the parametric-map generalization that the warm-start depends on.

> "If teacher finds wide but BC fails → **descriptor/coverage problem**."

This is the branch we are in. Teacher finds wide (R11.5R ✓); the parametric BC still fails held-out.
**The residual bottleneck is demo DENSITY / descriptor / policy-form — not basin width, not
reward-tuning, not RL.**

### Recommended next lever (a design decision, HALT for review)

Two concrete, non-RL levers, both consistent with the decision tree:

1. **Densify the (robust) demonstrations.** Multiple wide-basin θ per scenario + a finer scenario
   grid, so the parametric descriptor→θ map has enough coverage to generalize. Re-run this exact
   harness. (Directly attacks Cause 1, which is now the sole binding constraint.)
2. **Adopt a retrieval / nearest-robust-basin policy as the deployment form.** On B1 the
   nearest-schedule policy already generalizes better (.333) than the MLP (.167) and needs **no CEM
   and no oracle at deployment** — just a stored (descriptor, robust-θ) table + nearest lookup. This
   is a viable teacher-free deployment for the seen + near-distribution; densification would lift it
   further.

RL re-enters only after the map generalizes (lever 1) or as residual refinement on top of a
retrieval policy (lever 2) — not from the current warm-start.

## Caveats

- **Held-out is 12 scenarios** (7 dev + 5 test); rates move in coarse 1/7 / 1/5 steps. The run is
  **deterministic** (fixed seed, exact closed-loop rollouts), so these are not noisy estimates — but
  the caution about generalizing a conclusion from 12 held-out scenarios stands. The train signal
  (+6/44 ridge, mean-θ +0.159) is on firmer N.
- The gate **verdict** label is unchanged (`BC_REPRESENTATION_INSUFFICIENT` both arms) because
  `ridge_train < 0.80` still holds (0.523). The verdict label is coarse; the **decomposition** above
  is the actual finding, and it is not null — B1 strictly improves train fit and crude-policy
  held-out.
