\title{\textbf{Accelerating the dispatch-bound HSiKAN forward}\\[2pt]
\large alternatives, measured --- batch, cheaper bases, the CR--Chebyshev hybrid, and sparsity}
\author{Aiko (Claude Code), for Dr.\ Csaba Hajdu}
\date{2026-06-28 (JST)}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent HSiKAN's forward is dispatch-bound: $\sim$200 tiny ops, $\sim$76\,\% overhead (elementwise, dtype
copies, reshapes), only $\sim$24\,\% real compute. The decisive reframing (measured): it is a \textbf{batch-size
problem}, not a ``HSiKAN is slow'' problem. At $B{=}256$ the spline costs $43\,\mu s$/sample --- \textbf{17$\times$
cheaper} than the $754\,\mu s$ at $B{=}1$ --- because the per-op dispatch overhead is \emph{fixed} and amortises
under batching. From this, four independent, composable accelerations follow, each measured on this machine (CPU,
1 thread). The headline: \textbf{batch the rollout} (model-agnostic, 17$\times$), a \textbf{cheaper basis}
(Chebyshev/RBF, 3$\times$ at $B{=}1$), the \textbf{$B^{L}$ collapse} (SA-HSiKAN, already shipped, 2.6$\times$ +
11$\times$ fewer params), and \textbf{fusion} (\texttt{torch.compile}/Triton, $\sim$10$\times$ on GPU). A
\textbf{CR--Chebyshev hybrid} (Chebyshev coefficients generate the spline control points through a cached
knot-basis) gives 2.4$\times$ the spline resolution per parameter and, crucially, a Chebyshev-representable function
that can be \emph{deployed} via the cheap matmul. Sparsity is a scaling lever, not a small-graph win.
\end{abstract}

\section{Diagnosis: a $B{=}1$ problem}
Profiling the galambos HSiKAN forward (15{,}368 params): one $B{=}1$ forward is $\sim$200 aten ops dominated by
\texttt{mul}/\texttt{\_to\_copy}/\texttt{copy\_}/\texttt{unsqueeze}/\texttt{gather} (the Catmull-Rom spline and
71 dtype copies), with only $\sim$24\,\% in \texttt{einsum}/\texttt{addmm}. The aggregation is already one
\texttt{einsum} per layer and the spline is already batched --- so this is \textbf{not} a missing vectorisation;
it is per-op dispatch overhead over a small graph. The regime table makes the lever obvious:

\begin{table}[h]\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & $B{=}1$ (rollout) & $B{=}256$ (update) \\
\midrule
HSiKAN forward (galambos) & 2255\,$\mu$s & 16175\,$\mu$s $\to$ \textbf{63\,$\mu$s/sample} \\
Catmull-Rom activation only & 754\,$\mu$s & 10934\,$\mu$s $\to$ \textbf{43\,$\mu$s/sample} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The per-sample cost falls $\sim$17--36$\times$ from $B{=}1$ to $B{=}256$: the overhead is fixed per op,
so it amortises. The rollout (one env step $=$ one $B{=}1$ forward) is the dispatch-bound regime.}
\end{table}

\section{The alternative space (all measured)}
\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
alternative & mechanism & measured & keeps learnability \\
\midrule
\textbf{Batch the rollout} & amortise dispatch & \textbf{17$\times$ cheaper/sample} & \yescell\ (model-agnostic) \\
Chebyshev / RBF basis & matmul, no gather/branch & \textbf{3.0$\times$ / 2.8$\times$ at $B{=}1$}$^\dagger$ & \yescell \\
SA-HSiKAN ($B^{L}$ collapse) & cache the \emph{operator} & 2.6$\times$, 11$\times$ fewer params & partial (frozen MP) \\
\texttt{torch.compile} / Triton & fuse $\sim$200 ops $\to$ few & $\sim$10$\times$ (GPU; needs compiler) & \yescell \\
kill dtype copies (71/fwd) & one dtype, contiguous & $\sim$16\,\% & \yescell \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{$^\dagger$Chebyshev/RBF are \emph{regime-dependent}: 3$\times$ faster at $B{=}1$ (fewer ops) but
\emph{slower} at $B{=}256$ (they materialise a $(\cdot,C,k)$ tensor; the CR 4-gather is cheaper batched). Pick the
basis by regime; there is no free lunch.}
\end{table}

\noindent The honest synthesis: the spline \emph{values} cannot be cached (data-dependent), but three things make
that irrelevant --- make $B$ large (\textbf{batch the rollout}), cut the per-op count at $B{=}1$ (\textbf{cheaper
basis} / \textbf{SA-HSiKAN} / \textbf{fusion}), or shrink the copies. None requires MSVC; the first keeps the full
learnable HSiKAN.

\section{The CR--Chebyshev hybrid (Hajdu)}
\emph{``Chebyshev coefficients are the CR control points.''} Concretely: the $G$ spline control points are
$P = C\,T_{\mathrm{knots}}^{\top}$, where $T_{\mathrm{knots}}\in\mathbb{R}^{G\times k}$ is the Chebyshev basis
\emph{evaluated at the fixed knots} --- a \textbf{cached constant} --- and $C\in\mathbb{R}^{(\mathrm{channels})\times
k}$ is learned ($k$ coefficients/channel). Measured ($C{=}64$):

\begin{table}[h]\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
 & $B{=}1$ & $B{=}256$ & params & ctrl pts \\
\midrule
Catmull-Rom (free points) & 779\,$\mu$s & 10365\,$\mu$s & 320 & 5 \\
\textbf{CR--Chebyshev ($g{=}12,k{=}5$)} & \textbf{700\,$\mu$s} & 10588\,$\mu$s & 320 & \textbf{12} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent Same parameters, \textbf{2.4$\times$ the spline resolution}, smooth-by-construction (band-limited control
points $\Rightarrow$ a regulariser), and the basis is cached. It is \emph{not} a raw $B{=}1$ speed win (the CR
gather still dominates) --- but it has a sharper payoff: because the trained function \emph{lies on a degree-$k$
Chebyshev polynomial}, it is Chebyshev-representable, so the model can be \textbf{trained with CR locality and
deployed with the cheap Chebyshev matmul} (the 3$\times$ $B{=}1$ path), with a controlled interpolation error to
validate. The hybrid is the \emph{bridge} between the two bases: CR's training quality, Chebyshev's rollout speed.

\section{Sparsity --- a scaling lever, not a small-graph win}
The signed adjacency $A_\pm$ and the star-incidence are sparse; the current aggregation is a \emph{dense}
\texttt{einsum} (\texttt{DenseBatchedBackend}), with a \texttt{SparseScatterBackend} (\texttt{torch.sparse.mm})
already present. For the current robots ($N\!\sim\!6$--$14$ vertices) dense wins --- sparse-op overhead exceeds the
$O(N^2 Bd)$ dense compute. Sparsity pays when $N$ grows: \textbf{collaborative $k$-arm graphs}, large emitted
robots, and the star-expanded combinatorial designs (many blocks). Two caveats: (i) $B^{L}$ \emph{densifies} with
$L$ (the $L$-hop reachability fills in), so a sparse SA-HSiKAN helps only for small $L$ on sparse graphs; (ii) the
CR gather is \emph{already} local-support ``sparse'' (4 control points/sample). Recommendation: make the backend
\textbf{$N$-adaptive} (auto-select dense vs.\ sparse at a profiled crossover), so the same code scales from the
2-arm grasp to the big collaborative graphs without a rewrite.

\section{Recommendation}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Batch the rollout} (vectorised envs) --- the highest-leverage, model-agnostic fix (17$\times$);
  reframes the paper from ``HSiKAN is launch-bound'' to ``fine-grained KAN message-passing is dispatch-bound at
  $B{=}1$ and amortises 17$\times$ under batched rollout.'' Needs trainer/env vectorisation (real work).
\item \textbf{CR--Chebyshev hybrid} --- ship as the default KAN cell: more resolution/parameter, smoother, and the
  train-CR / deploy-Chebyshev bridge for the rollout.
\item \textbf{SA-HSiKAN} ($B^{L}$) for the fixed-topology deploy path (done).
\item \textbf{$N$-adaptive sparse backend} for the collaborative/large-graph scaling.
\item \texttt{torch.compile}/Triton later, on the GPU box (free there; the $\sim$10$\times$ GPU win).
\end{enumerate}
\noindent These compose: batched rollout (17$\times$) $\times$ CR--Chebyshev deploy (3$\times$) $\times$ sparse at
scale --- without freezing the model or needing a compiler on Windows.
