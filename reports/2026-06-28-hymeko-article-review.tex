\section{Summary \& readiness}

HyMeKo is presented as a hub-and-spoke MDE architecture. A single authoritative, signed/typed hypergraph intermediate representation (IR) --- built on Levi/Berge incidence with a Blake3 content hash --- replaces the usual mesh of pairwise format converters. The IR is manipulated through three operations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{compile} (text $\rightarrow$ IR),
  \item \textbf{project} (IR $\rightarrow$ sparse tensor),
  \item \textbf{emit} (IR $\rightarrow$ target text).
\end{itemize}

\noindent The paper claims four algebraic properties, each backed by a proof \emph{sketch} in the appendices:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item alias invariance,
  \item content-addressability,
  \item projection--emission independence,
  \item bounded storage overhead.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Validation.} One IR emits six robot formats (URDF, SDF, Gazebo, MJCF, DOT, Mermaid) at sub-millisecond per robot, reported as $100$--$700\times$ faster than the \texttt{xacro}\,$\rightarrow$\,\texttt{gz sdf}\,$\rightarrow$\,\texttt{mujoco} subprocess stack, alongside a capability gap (the MuJoCo importer fails on long kinematic chains).

\noindent\textbf{State.} Late-stage, near submission-ready. The document compiles clean and all 37 bibliography entries resolve. The prior reviewer's hard contradiction on storage overhead ($\rho < 0.7$ asserted in prose vs.\ $\rho = 2.00$ in the table) has been \emph{fixed}: property A4 now reports $\rho$ improving $2.00 \rightarrow 1.01$.

\section{Technical errors (precise)}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Wrong proposition cited.} Cross-view value agreement is attributed to Proposition~3 (projection--emission independence, a cost-factoring statement). It should follow from Proposition~1 (alias invariance) applied per shared query. (P4)
  \item \textbf{``Idempotent'' mislabels a round-trip identity.} $\text{compile}\circ\text{lift}=\mathrm{id}_H$ is a left-inverse / round-trip property, not idempotence ($f\circ f=f$). (P8)
  \item \textbf{$103\times$ vs $120\times$.} The headline speed-up at $|V|{=}1000$ is arithmetically inconsistent with the paper's own table ($34.585\,\text{ms}\rightarrow 4136.6\,\text{ms} = 120\times$). (P1)
  \item \textbf{Figure integer inconsistency.} In \file{fig\_pairwise\_vs\_hub.tex}, caption, drawn box count, and the left-panel grid disagree ($9$ vs $8$ vs $10$). (P5)
  \item \textbf{Unsupported capability-gap claim.} The MuJoCo-FAIL gap (and the $|V|{=}5000$ ``$700\times$'') has no supporting in-body table row. (P2)
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{On the propositions overall.} The four ``propositions'' are honest proof-sketches-by-inspection, and the paper now says so. The storage-overhead proof (A4) is the most rigorous of the four and is correct.

\section{Top 3 highest-leverage improvements}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Restore Prop.~1's proof to the body} (or inline its lemmas) so the correctness story is self-contained. This is the single most important fix: A2/A3 depend on Prop.~1, so exiling it to supplementary leaves the visible appendices unable to stand alone.
  \item \textbf{Make every headline number match its table.} Correct $103\times \rightarrow 120\times$, and add the $|V|{=}5000$ and FAIL rows. Quantitative inconsistency erodes trust in \emph{all} the reported numbers.
  \item \textbf{Align ``by construction / impossible'' language with what the propositions actually prove}, and fix the Prop.~3\,$\rightarrow$\,Prop.~1 miscitation for cross-view value agreement.
\end{enumerate}

Near submission-ready. The required changes are surgical fixes, not restructuring. None requires re-running the benchmark --- except, optionally, pulling the already-collected $|V|{=}2000/5000$ CSV rows into the table.
